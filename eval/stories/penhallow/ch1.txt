The Last Harvest at Penhallow

Chapter One

Iris Calder came home to Penhallow on the first Monday of October, because Mr. Osei had died on Sunday night. She was twenty-eight, with black hair tied back in a knot and green eyes that her mother had always said missed nothing. She had not been back to the orchard in two years.

Her brother Dominic was waiting at the station in his blue Ford truck. He had a pale scar that ran through his left eyebrow, from a fall off the barn roof when he was twelve, and it made him look permanently doubtful. He took her bag without a word and swung it into the back.

"Fenna's been cooking since dawn," he said. "Half the valley will be at the house tonight."

The orchard house stood at the top of the lane. Their aunt Fenna, the older sister of their late mother, met them at the door with flour on her hands. She was sixty-one, grey-haired and broad-shouldered, and she had run the farm alone since Iris's father died. Biscuit, the old spaniel with the grey muzzle, pushed past her to lean on Iris's legs.

Over supper they talked about Mr. Osei. He had been the neighbour on the east side of the orchard for forty years. He had lent them his ladders and his tractor, and he had taught Dominic to graft apple trees. He had died quietly in his chair, with the radio still playing. The funeral would be on Tuesday at the chapel on the hill.

Iris looked out of the window at the dark rows of trees. When she was nine, the big barn had been painted red, and she remembered lying on its roof with Dominic and counting stars. Now the barn was a flaking grey, and half its doors hung open.

"Is it true what Mr. Hale wrote?" she asked. "That he wants to buy us out?"

Fenna put down her fork. "He has made an offer," she said. "A good one. We have until the end of the month to answer."

Nobody spoke for a while. Biscuit sighed under the table, and the clock in the hall struck ten.
