Chapter Three: The Morning After

By Wednesday morning the storm had passed, and the sea lay flat and grey as a plate. Nell Harker, thirty-nine years old and very tired, stood at the cellar door with a bucket. Ten years before, when she was twenty-four, she had stood in this same doorway watching her father's boat go out, and she remembered thinking that the harbour had never looked so small.

She unlocked the cellar with the brass key and found the casks dry. Behind her, in the best chair by the fire, Gideon Harker poured the tea for everyone, grumbling that the stove had gone out again and that nobody in this house knew how to keep a fire.

Nell looked up from the lock with her brown eyes and did not say anything. There was a great deal she could have said.

Maud came in with her copper hair tied back and a basket of eggs from the village. She said the doctor would be back by noon and that the Curlew had come through the night with only a scraped keel.

Tobias was already in the yard. He had refused to stay in bed, and now he stood in the cold light splitting a stack of logs for the stove, swinging the heavy axe with both arms until the sweat ran down his face. The scar on the back of his right hand whitened as he gripped the handle.

"You'll do yourself harm," Nell called.

"I've done myself worse," he said, and brought the axe down again.

She went back inside and set out the cups. The tea had gone cold. The fire had burned down to a few red coals. Outside, the gulls had come back to the wall, and the sea made its small flat sound against the stones, as if nothing had happened at all.
