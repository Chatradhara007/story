Chapter Two: The Storm

On Tuesday the wind rose before breakfast, and by noon the sea was climbing the harbour wall. Maud, who had dyed her black hair copper in the village the week before just to annoy her grandfather, stood at the window and said the Curlew would be smashed on her slip.

Tobias went down with a rope. Nell watched from the door as he climbed the ladder to the boat's deck, and she saw the ladder slide away beneath him. He fell hard onto the stones and lay there. When Nell and Maud reached him, the scar on the back of his right hand stood out white against the wet stone, and his right arm was bent wrong below the elbow. Maud, the elder of the two sisters by two years, took charge in the way she always did in a crisis, shouting for a cart.

The doctor from the village arrived within the hour. The bone was broken in two places, he said, and he bound the arm in splints and a sling and told Tobias to keep it still for six weeks.

That night, with the storm at its worst, Gideon Harker died in his sleep in the best chair by the fire. Nell found him at midnight, his hands folded as if he had arranged them himself, the fire gone cold. Maud cried loudly. Nell did not cry at all, which frightened her more than crying would have.

It was Tobias, one-armed and white with pain, who took the brass key from its nail, because water was coming in under the cellar door and somebody had to reach the casks. But the old lock had swollen with the damp. He wrenched at it until his temper broke, and then, cursing, he hurled the brass key out through the doorway into the harbour, where it sank at once in the black water.

Nell stood on the steps with the lantern and understood that it was the only key the cellar had ever had.
