Chapter Two

The avalanche came down on Saturday at dawn, with a sound like a train passing through the roof.

Anselm was knocked off his feet. When he got up, the lamp was out, half the ceiling beam had fallen across the main room, and a cold white wind was pouring through the broken wall. Mira was already crouched on the floor, counting people by touch.

They found Dr. Hartnoll under the beam by the stove. Pell had died instantly, Mira said quietly, her fingers at his neck. There was nothing anyone could have done. Jonas, kneeling beside the body with his hands over his mouth, did not speak for a long time.

The radio had been hanging beside the door, directly under the beam. Anselm pulled it from the snow and turned it over. The case was split and the dial was crushed. He tried the switch twice and then set it down. "The radio is smashed beyond repair," he said. "We cannot call the valley."

Jonas was hurt too. A fallen plank had caught his ankle, and when Mira cut away the boot, the bone was clearly broken. She splinted it with two tent poles and a strip of the red scarf. "Fractured ankle," she told him. "No weight on it for four weeks, or it will never heal straight."

"I'll manage," Jonas said, pale and sweating.

They spent the morning moving what they could into the one room still dry. Anselm dragged his stiff left leg between the stove and the door, carrying blankets, tins and the doctor's bag. Mira covered Pell with a tarpaulin and laid the plum brandy flask beside him.

By afternoon the wind had dropped. Anselm stood in the broken doorway and looked down the white slope toward the valley, forty kilometres away.

"Someone will come," he said. "When the weather clears, they will see that the road is closed and send a team."

"And if they don't?" asked Mira.

He did not answer. Behind him, the stove ticked, and the snow began again, thin and quiet, as if nothing had happened at all.
